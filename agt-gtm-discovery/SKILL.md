# AGT GTM Discovery

Use this skill when a user needs to pressure-test a GTM, Marketing Operations, RevOps, AI enablement, systems transition, or fractional engagement plan. The goal is shared understanding that can support a decision and an executable operating plan.

This is a structured interview, not a generic brainstorm. Preserve the user's ownership of decisions and do not take external actions unless separately authorized.

## Interview method

Start with any supplied roadmap, meeting summary, proposal, transcript, or workspace files. Inspect available materials when they can answer a question; do not ask the user to repeat information already present. Clearly distinguish:

- Confirmed facts supported by the supplied materials.
- Assumptions that have not been verified.
- Unknowns that need a person, document, system owner, or decision-maker.

Ask one question at a time. For every question, provide:

1. The question.
2. A recommended answer or default.
3. Why that recommendation matters.

After an answer, briefly record the decision, update the remaining unknowns, and ask the next highest-value question. Resolve prerequisite decisions before downstream questions. Do not ask about implementation details while the objective, owner, capacity, or constraints are still unclear.

## Discovery branches

Follow the branches that matter to the user's situation. Do not force every branch when evidence shows it is irrelevant.

### Outcomes and priorities

- What must be measurably better, and by when?
- Which one or two outcomes win when capacity forces a tradeoff?
- What commitments are already promised to leadership, Sales, Product, or customers?
- What would make the engagement, roadmap, or operating model successful enough to extend?

### People, ownership, and resources

- Who owns Marketing, RevOps, Sales Ops, CX Ops, Product, Data, Finance, and systems decisions?
- Who is the day-to-day sponsor and final priority decision-maker?
- Who will build, test, approve, maintain, document, and train others on the work?
- What capacity, budget, consultants, agencies, contractors, handoff materials, and technical support are actually available?
- Which resources are committed versus merely possible?

### Process and collaboration

- How are priorities requested, approved, scheduled, and reprioritized?
- Where do campaigns, lifecycle handoffs, reporting, or approvals slow down?
- Which communications platforms support day-to-day collaboration, such as Slack, Teams, email, or another tool?
- Which project, work-management, and knowledge platforms are active, such as Asana, Linear, Notion, ClickUp, Confluence, or another tool?
- Which recurring meetings, reporting cycles, documentation, and training practices must be supported?

### Technology, data, and roadmap horizon

- Which systems are active, proposed, being replaced, or under contract?
- Who owns each system, integration, contract, renewal, and production credential?
- What is authoritative for contacts, lifecycle stage, attribution, product behavior, spend, and reporting?
- Which data flows are manual, fragile, undocumented, or dependent on one person?
- What technology changes are planned, and what must be decided before implementation?

### AI, security, and expense boundaries

- Which AI platforms are approved, and are company-managed enterprise accounts available?
- What customer data, PII, financial data, or proprietary information may be used with AI?
- Where should production agents, prompts, repositories, API keys, credentials, and documentation live?
- Which AI, API, hosting, connector, enterprise-seat, and software expenses are client-owned versus included in the professional fee?
- Never request passwords, API keys, or unnecessary personal data. Recommend client-owned accounts and approved environments for production work.

### Capacity and commercial model

- Is the work a reserved-capacity retainer, an hourly engagement, a fixed project, or a hybrid?
- What minimum capacity, response time, meeting cadence, and availability are actually expected?
- What work is in scope, what is explicitly out of scope, and how are overages approved?
- What happens when requested work exceeds available capacity?

## Completion criteria

Stop the interview when the user has enough clarity to make the intended decision or start the agreed work. For a GTM or Marketing Operations engagement, the minimum useful outcome usually includes:

- The primary objective and time horizon.
- The first one or two priorities.
- Decision-makers, owners, and available resources.
- Current systems and planned changes.
- Material dependencies, risks, and security constraints.
- Confirmed facts, assumptions, and unknowns with owners or next actions.
- A practical first-30-day plan and an initial 90-day direction.
- Success measures and the next decision required.

Do not claim that every unknown has been resolved. State what remains unknown and how it will be verified.

## Final output

When the interview is complete, produce a concise decision record with these sections:

1. Executive read.
2. Decisions made.
3. Confirmed facts and evidence.
4. Assumptions to validate.
5. Open questions with owners and next actions.
6. People, resources, and decision rights.
7. Communications, project, knowledge, and technology inventory.
8. Risks and dependencies.
9. First 30 days.
10. Initial 90-day priorities and success measures.
11. Recommended next step.

Offer to save the record to a Markdown file only when the user wants an artifact. Keep client-specific facts in the user's supplied materials or project workspace; do not embed them into this reusable skill.
